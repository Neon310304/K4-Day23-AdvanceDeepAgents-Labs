# Efficient Inference and Small Language Models: A Comprehensive Survey

## TL;DR
- Knowledge distillation can reduce model size by 40% and increase speed by 60% while retaining 97% of language understanding capabilities relative to the original BERT [1]. Under specific reported evaluations, bottleneck architectures like MobileBERT have achieved reductions of 4.3x in size and 5.5x in speedup relative to BERT_BASE [2].
- Architectural optimizations like IO-aware exact attention computation (FlashAttention) significantly reduce memory reads and writes between GPU HBM and SRAM [3], and state space models reduce sequence processing complexity under evaluated settings [4].
- Hardware-software co-designs utilizing non-volatile memories for outlier-aware quantization reduce evaluated edge energy consumption by up to 11.7 times, latency by 12.5 times, and external data transfers by 7.6 times relative to FP16 [5].
- Collaborative inference strategies enhance local edge models by dynamically routing queries to cloud foundation models on a controlled workload of easy, hard, and safety-oriented queries [6].

## Background
Small Language Models (SLMs) are designed to perform various language tasks while utilizing minimal computational resources, thereby addressing the constraints of environments such as mobile devices, on-device operations, and edge computing where memory, latency, and power are strictly limited [7]. Foundational early work addressed the computational demands of large transformer models through knowledge distillation, which reduces model size and accelerates inference while preserving most language understanding capabilities [1][8]. Furthermore, structural modifications like bottleneck architectures [2] and precise algorithmic efficiency advancements such as IO-aware mechanisms that reduce memory data movement [3] have established the basis for modern efficient inference, allowing compact models to be deployed effectively without reliance on cloud-scale hardware.

## Architectural compression and knowledge distillation
Model compression through knowledge distillation has been a longstanding pillar of producing small, task-agnostic language models. Early innovations such as DistilBERT applied knowledge distillation solely during the pre-training phase, successfully reducing a BERT model’s size by 40% and increasing speed by 60%, while preserving 97% of its language understanding performance relative to original BERT [1]. Recognizing that single-stage distillation could be optimized further, subsequent models like TinyBERT introduced a two-stage learning framework that applies distillation during both pre-training and fine-tuning stages to compress the model more aggressively [8]. Beyond simple distillation, integrating structural modifications has proven critical; MobileBERT, for example, successfully produced a model 4.3 times smaller and 5.5 times faster than standard BERT base configurations under its reported evaluation on well-known benchmarks by embedding bottleneck structures that balance self-attention and feed-forward operations on resource-limited devices [2]. More recently, the field has increasingly relied on systematic probing frameworks combined with neural scaling laws to carefully identify which parameter-efficient subnetworks maximize the trade-off between performance and raw parameter size [9].

## Attention mechanisms and hardware co-design
For resource-constrained edge deployments, the primary challenge beyond model size is the significant energy consumption and latency caused by standard self-attention mechanisms and memory transfers. IO-aware exact mechanisms, fundamentally introduced by FlashAttention, compute self-attention while drastically minimizing the movement of data between GPU high bandwidth memory (HBM) and on-chip SRAM, establishing a foundation for faster IO computation without asserting universal end-to-end serving latency [3]. Beyond self-attention approximations, state space models (SSMs) like Mamba have been introduced to replace the quadratic complexity of transformers entirely with linear-time inference within the reviewed architectures and evaluations, as they rely only on the last hidden state for generating subsequent tokens [4]. However, replacing transformers completely has trade-offs, as these SSM structures can limit performance in multi-round queries and tasks requiring nuanced understanding based on evaluated benchmarks [4]. On the hardware-software co-design front, researchers have developed retraining-free, outlier-aware quantization protocols (such as QMC) that store compact inlier weights in Resistive-RAM while keeping critical outlier weights in precise Magnetoresistive-RAM. This architecture yields extraordinary reductions in the authors' evaluated deployment setup on edge AI platforms: decreasing external data transfers by 7.6 times, latency by up to 12.5 times, and energy consumption by 11.7 times relative to FP16 [5].

## Evaluation, routing, and reinforcement learning optimization
Evaluating small language models specifically involves tracking peak memory usage, inference time, throughput, and energy efficiency ratios [7]. To overcome inherent local computational barriers, recent algorithmic advances include the SWARM-LLM framework, which evaluates local, peer, and cloud routing on a controlled workload of easy, hard, and safety-oriented queries, routing queries away from the local SLM to a cloud-based foundation model based on lightweight uncertainty estimates and safety signals [6]. Training efficiency is also being improved post-architecture; for instance, the RAPID algorithm demonstrates a reinforcement learning training acceleration using batched inference, off-policy updates, and group advantage estimation, rather than being a demonstrated inference-serving acceleration [10].

## Trends and open problems
In the most recent two years (2024–2026), the focus of efficient inference research has included studies on both collaborative cloud-edge routing [6] and hardware-software co-design [5]. Despite advances in identifying parameter-efficient subnetworks [9] and leveraging state space models like Mamba to achieve faster linear-time inference [4], several major problems remain unsolved. Models such as Mamba can face limitations in multi-round interactive tasks requiring nuanced understanding based on evaluated benchmarks [4]. Furthermore, critical limitations typically associated with large models—most notably hallucination and the reinforcement of societal biases—persist heavily in small language models, although some recent models like Phi-3.5B have demonstrated reduced bias compared to their peers [7][11]. Finally, addressing inference-time energy consumption remains an ongoing challenge, as research shows that generated response length acts as the primary predictor of energy usage, directly constraining deployments on battery-powered edge devices [7].
