# Survey on Video and Multimodal Generation: Foundations, Latent Dynamics, and Open Problems

## TL;DR
- Diffusion models have largely replaced GANs as the state-of-the-art in multimodal and video generation, bolstered by large Vision-Language Models for enhanced semantic fidelity [1][2]. Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) introduced powerful denoising synthesis [3], while Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) later applied the diffusion process in an autoencoder latent space to retain quality while reducing complexity [4].
- Despite reaching over 13 billion parameters in models like HunyuanVideo [5], diffusion architectures face severe computational inefficiencies due to iterative sampling latency and lack interpretability [2].
- Temporal consistency in long-form generation remains challenging, driving the field to adopt latent models coupled with transformer backbones to capture spatiotemporal dependencies [6].
- Standard diffusion models often prioritize visual alignment over kinematic plausibility, producing physically impossible deformations in some settings because they do not explicitly model underlying physics [7][8].
- Researchers are attempting to address physical extrapolation by explicitly modeling latent dynamics, such as casting latent transitions as explicit kinematic numerical integrations, which improves physical extrapolation in controlled benchmarks [8].

## Background
Video and multimodal generation involves the synthesis of realistic, temporally coherent, and contextually appropriate visual content from text, audio, and image inputs. This field is foundational for creating rich AI-generated content (AIGC), extending traditional image synthesis to complex dynamic scenarios. Modern frameworks conceptually unify these modalities using principles like Information Bottleneck and Partial Information Decomposition to efficiently share and complement data [9]. The capability to generate and control dynamic physical scenes is becoming essential for applications ranging from creative industries to simulated "world models" in robotic visuomotor learning [10]. Consequently, ensuring semantic fidelity and temporal alignment across generated modalities is currently a critical area of focus [11][1]. Foundational advancements such as Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) [3], which utilize denoising score matching with Langevin dynamics, and Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) [4], which perform diffusion in a compressed autoencoder latent space, have been pivotal in shaping current architectures.

## Foundational transitions in architectures
Early approaches to generative AI relied heavily on frameworks like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) [2]. To bridge multimodal data types, foundational models commonly utilized encoder-decoder architectures, matching cross-aligned encoders to create a joint representation space [12]. However, since the emergence of models like Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) [3], the video generation landscape has radically shifted toward diffusion-based generative processes [1][2]. DDPM provided high-quality image synthesis using denoising score matching, but operated directly in pixel space. To address computational limitations, Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) utilize pretrained autoencoders for latent compression, applying the diffusion process in this compressed semantic space for high visual quality and improved computational efficiency [4]. Thus, VAEs remain critical as latent compressors, while diffusion models offer unparalleled visual quality and have largely supplanted GANs [1]. In parallel, incorporating large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into these video architectures has significantly enhanced the models' semantic fidelity when interpreting multimodal text and image prompts [1][2].

## Scaling video models and computational bottlenecks
The past two years have seen immense scaling of video diffusion paradigms. Open-source foundations have caught up with leading closed-source architectures; for instance, the HunyuanVideo model, with over 13 billion parameters, achieved superior performance against major competitors in its original authors' evaluation [5]. However, this scaling brings substantial drawbacks. A critical barrier to the deployment of these Diffusion Models is their computational inefficiency, largely due to the high latency introduced by multi-step iterative sampling procedures [2]. Furthermore, unlike earlier GAN architectures, the denoising steps remain a "black box," severely limiting the interpretability of the generation process [2]. This inefficiency prompts a growing need to balance scaling with alternative sampling techniques or fundamentally distinct generative families.

## From spatiotemporal consistency to latent dynamics
While raw visual fidelity in short clips is exceptionally high, preserving temporal and spatiotemporal consistency remains one of the largest engineering hurdles in video generation [11]. Researchers note that generating long videos with consistency across multiple subjects is a persistent issue for standard diffusion models [1]. To address this, the dominant approach is to perform generation within a latent feature space rather than at the pixel or patch level [6]. 

Recent strategies enhance this by integrating Latent Image and Latent Video Diffusion pathways, which improves temporal coherence directly alongside visual quality [13]. However, certain models operating via standard diffusion without explicit physics modeling can struggle with kinematic accuracy [8]. This means that while they preserve superficial consistency, standard methods often prioritize visual alignment over kinematic plausibility, resulting in physically impossible deformations when generating complex motion in specific scenarios [7].

Consequently, there is a prominent shift toward "world model" thinking that explicitly models latent dynamics [8]. The Chain of World approach employs a video VAE to explicitly disentangle each sequence into separate structure and motion representations, thereby compacting the temporal knowledge required for frame prediction [10]. Other research explicitly introduces distributions over per-particle latent actions, processed by causal spatio-temporal transformers to better simulate stochastic multi-object interactions [14]. More radically, frameworks like Latent Dynamics Reasoning (LDR) cast latent transitions as explicit kinematic numerical integrations, which improves the physical extrapolation of models over long rollouts, demonstrating a massive reduction in error on controlled physics benchmarks [8].

## Trends and open problems
Over the past two years, video generation has clearly shifted from basic short-form clip generation toward scalable latent diffusion methods [11][1]. A major open problem is the persistent conflict between generative fidelity and human-intent alignment, as error accumulation and motion-appearance coupling degrade adherence to user prompts over long video durations [11]. Another significant issue is the difficulty in capturing complex physical dynamics. While state-of-the-art architectures can generate visually stunning sequences, their limitations in understanding real-world physics in certain settings can lead to impossible deformations [7]. As a result, the integration of explicitly modeled kinematic dynamics—such as structured numerical integration in the latent space—is viewed as a crucial research direction to evolve video generation from mere visual prediction into more accurate extrapolative world modeling in controlled environments [10][8].
