# World Models: From Latent Dynamics and Imagination to Interactive Simulation and Reliable Control

## TL;DR
- World models are predictive representations, not necessarily pixel generators: PlaNet plans in latent space, Dreamer learns through imagined trajectories, and MuZero predicts planning-relevant quantities without requiring observation reconstruction.[1][2][3]
- Recent interactive generation and foundation-model platforms broaden simulation interfaces, but navigable visuals and proposed planning applications do not establish reliable action-conditioned control.[4][5][6]
- Planning needs checks beyond terminal goal proximity and ordinary simulation scores: partial-replay driving evaluation and inverse-dynamics action consistency expose failures that appearance can conceal.[7][8]
- Physical fidelity, closed-loop success, and out-of-distribution object dynamics are complementary evaluation targets; recent studies do not support treating any single score as general world understanding.[6][9][10]

## Background
This survey uses **world model** as a working definition: a learned predictive representation of an environment's evolution, often conditioned on actions, that can support simulation, behavior learning, or planning. This is a synthesis rather than a universally agreed definition: World Models learns compressed spatial and temporal representations, while MuZero learns reward, policy, and value predictions without requiring reconstruction of observations or the true environment state.[11][3] Predictive Representations of State provides an earlier conceptual foundation: state can be represented by predictions of selected action–observation tests sufficient to determine other test predictions. That work explicitly studies prediction rather than reward-based control; the connection to contemporary neural world models is conceptual, not evidence that they implement classical predictive state representations.[12]

The scope is AI world models, emphasizing visual environments and embodied decision-making. Foundational sources precede the requested recent window, **2024-10-11–2026-10-11**, using the user-supplied current date of 2026-10-11 in Asia/Bangkok. Selected dated recent evidence spans August 2025 to October 8, 2026; it is not an exhaustive account of the window.[4][7][10] Why the distinction between prediction and control matters is already clear in foundational model-based reinforcement learning: composing imperfect predictions can amplify errors, and one-step prediction error need not track planning usefulness.[13]

## Representation objectives: reconstructive, task-directed, and predictive latent states
A useful comparison separates **what the model predicts** from **how an agent uses it**. The original World Models project separates vision, memory, and controller: a VAE compresses observations, while a probabilistic recurrent model predicts latent codes. The controller is trained separately from the representation and dynamics components.[15] PlaNet likewise learns a compact predictive dynamics model, but combines deterministic and stochastic transitions and uses latent overshooting as a multi-step training objective. Its observation model supplies a training signal rather than being used directly during planning.[1]

MuZero illustrates a different objective. Its recurrent hidden state receives a hypothetical next action, and its predictions target reward, action-selection policy, and value. The paper explicitly does not require the hidden state to reconstruct the original observation or match the unknown true environment state.[3] Thus a model useful for decisions need not be a visually faithful general simulator; conversely, reconstructing appearance is not by itself evidence that the model preserves every decision-relevant variable.[1][3]

Recent latent-prediction work makes the second point explicit. Toward Physically Grounded JEPA World Models for Goal-Conditioned Robotic Planning, submitted to arXiv on September 3, 2026 (the HF page displays September 2), predicts without reconstructing future pixels, but argues that latent prediction alone does not ensure retention of control-relevant information. Its proposed inverse-dynamics objective makes transitions informative about actions, while physical-state alignment grounds consecutive representations in configuration and motion.[14] Interpretation: representation adequacy should be assessed relative to the intended interventions and decisions, not inferred merely from whether the model generates pixels or predicts embeddings.[3][14]

## Using imagined futures: online planning versus learned behavior
Prediction and decision-making are distinct design choices. PlaNet uses model-predictive control with cross-entropy-method action-sequence optimization, replanning at each step; its retrieved method description says it does not use a policy or value network.[1] Dreamer's original project abstract instead describes learning behavior by propagating analytic gradients of learned state values through imagined compact-state trajectories.[2] MuZero performs Monte Carlo Tree Search at each timestep over its learned planning-relevant model.[3] These approaches therefore differ in both training and decision interfaces; the evidence does not justify arranging them in a universal performance hierarchy.[1][2][3]

Imagination also creates a failure mode: a controller can exploit imperfections in the generated environment. The original World Models project adjusts a model temperature parameter to address exploitation in its experiments, but that intervention is not a general solution to model mismatch.[15] PlaNet identifies accumulated multi-step errors and overconfidence outside the training distribution as challenges, while the foundational self-correcting-model paper distinguishes composed-prediction behavior from one-step prediction error.[1][13]

ACID, published July 2, 2026, addresses a related decision-time issue: judging a candidate only by predicted terminal distance to the goal leaves intermediate transition realizability unchecked. It uses an inverse-dynamics model to infer the action from each predicted transition and penalizes disagreement with the action originally supplied to the world model, using an adaptive planning-cost weight.[8] Its abstract reports improvements across four action-conditioned models and six tasks, with reduced planning computation needed to match baseline accuracy; the retrieved evidence does not establish exact savings, uncertainty, or a cross-paper compute ranking.[8] Interpretation: correcting the decision criterion and improving the learned dynamics are complementary avenues, not interchangeable demonstrations that long-horizon error has been solved.[8][13]

## Interactive generation and foundation models: interfaces versus demonstrated utility
Genie 3's August 5, 2025 announcement reports prompt-generated worlds navigable at 24 frames per second, with consistency for a few minutes at 720p. These are official announcement claims, not independently reproduced benchmark measurements.[4] Researcher-retrieved search excerpts from the announcement describe constrained direct actions, multi-agent challenges, and an interaction duration of minutes rather than hours; the independent citation review could not recheck the direct-action and multi-agent limitations because its page retrieval truncated before that section. Those limitations remain excerpt-supported rather than independently verified here. The independently accessible capability description establishes bounded visual navigation, not unrestricted environmental intervention or reliable task completion.[4]

Cosmos broadens the modeling interface in a different way. Its retrieved paper describes diffusion and autoregressive transformers using continuous and discrete video latents, respectively, followed by specialized conditioning or post-training. Camera-pose conditioning supports viewpoint navigation; robotic video–action sequences support action-specific prediction. The paper's retrieved discussion also contemplates action commands, trajectories, and instructions as inputs.[5] These are distinct conditioning choices: moving a virtual camera is not the same intervention as manipulating an object with a robot.[5]

Crucially, the retrieved Cosmos 'Future Cosmos' discussion describes evaluating simulated action sequences with an external cost/reward module, but explicitly says the paper does not include empirical application results for those listed possibilities. This caveat applies to that discussion, not to every experiment elsewhere in the paper, whose later sections were not fully inspected.[5] World-in-World supplies a complementary empirical perspective: its project summary reports that appealing text-prompted video generation provides limited low-level control and only modest embodied-task help without adaptation; action-conditioned post-training improves action–motion alignment and task success in its tested settings.[6] The evidence supports distinguishing pretrained visual capacity, action-conditioned adaptation, and verified downstream benefit rather than conflating them.[5][6]

## Evaluation: closed-loop usefulness, intervention sensitivity, and physical diagnostics
Evaluation should preserve several separate questions. Does prediction remain useful when composed? Does the simulator respond appropriately when some agents are constrained? Can decisions selected from imagined rollouts succeed when executed? Does generated motion obey specified physical relationships? These questions are motivated by different failure evidence and cannot be answered by one appearance score.[13][7][6][9]

Beyond Simulation, published August 3, 2025, analyzes the Waymo Open Sim-Agents Challenge metametric and extends evaluation to partial replay and agents causally relevant to the ego vehicle. Its abstract reports scenarios where strong unperturbed simulation performers fail when the ego agent must replay the original trajectory. No failure counts or statistical effect sizes were retrieved; the finding supports a scoped intervention-sensitivity warning, not a universal rejection of simulation metrics.[7] World-in-World instead evaluates four closed-loop environments, prioritizing task success: a selected decision is executed, new observations arrive, and the agent can replan. That operational test measures something different from a video's standalone quality.[6]

World Models' Last Exam in Physics, dated October 6, 2026, pairs initial images and prompts with predefined physical criteria rather than requiring reference videos. Its abstract reports **40 tasks**, **eight video-generation models**, and **1,280 videos**, with the best tested model scoring **57.76/100** overall. These are author-reported benchmark results; detailed uncertainty and complete rankings were not inspected.[9] The paper expressly limits assessments to observable relationships under stated experimental assumptions: passing observability screening does not establish correctness, and unmeasured properties remain unverified. The score is therefore not a calibrated measure of general physical or causal understanding.[9]

Interpretation: useful reporting should combine closed-loop outcomes with diagnostic tests, action/intervention sensitivity, and explicit conditions of measurement. Task success answers an operational question; targeted physical tests help locate failures without themselves demonstrating end-to-end control utility.[7][6][9]

## Generalization and object interactions: why scale alone is an incomplete answer
The original latent-planning literature already warns about overconfident predictions outside the training distribution.[1] More recent evidence makes generalization more diagnostic. What 30,000 Hours of Ego-centric Video Does Not Teach, dated October 8, 2026, separates agent motion from the agent's effect on objects and evaluates an out-of-distribution benchmark with different capture hardware, sites, and participants.[10]

In that controlled study, increasing training data by 100× improves agent and object modeling unevenly: agent fidelity improves more strongly, while object fidelity remains lower and improves slowly. The authors report that skeleton-conditioned training at 1,000 hours exceeds the agent fidelity reached by standard conditioning at 30,000 hours, but these quantities compare that study's conditioning designs, not all architectures or data distributions.[10] An object-centric adaptive noise-scheduling intervention raises the object-fidelity level reached, yet the paper describes the improvement as partial. The retrieved introduction and abstract support the scoped claim, not a fully audited account of its ablations or mechanism.[10]

This evidence complements, rather than directly ranks against, the physically grounded JEPA proposal. The JEPA abstract reports that adding physical-state alignment improves planning success over inverse dynamics alone across its four benchmark tasks; the ego-centric study instead diagnoses a remaining object-interaction gap under scaling and targeted supervision.[14][10] The datasets, model objectives, and metrics differ, so the supported synthesis is that control-relevant representation and localized object dynamics deserve explicit evaluation—not that either method is universally superior or that data scaling is useless.[14][10]

## Trends and open problems
Within the retrieved recent window, the central change is a widening of scope: interactive visual worlds and foundation-model conditioning coexist with more explicit tests of planning, action consistency, physical relationships, and object dynamics.[4][5][7][8][9][10] This is a thematic interpretation of selected evidence, not a bibliometric claim or proof of a field-wide consensus. The latest dated retained source is October 8, 2026; the search does not establish complete coverage through October 11 or of the early months of the requested window.[10]

**Reliable long-horizon decisions remain open.** Compounding error is a foundational concern; restricted-duration interactive generation and recent intermediate-action consistency checks provide no general guarantee against it.[13][4][8] Proposed research direction (interpretation): evaluate uncertainty and error recovery together with executed success over varied horizons rather than reporting terminal prediction quality alone.[1][8][13]

**Action conditioning is not the same as causal fidelity.** Partial replay reveals failures concealed by ordinary simulation evaluation, while constrained direct actions in interactive generation limit which interventions can be tested.[7][4] Proposed research direction (interpretation): report which interventions the model actually accepts, and test sensitivity to constrained or independently controlled agents before claiming suitability as a policy-training environment.[7]

**Physical and out-of-distribution validation must remain scoped.** Observable physical tests leave unmeasured properties unverified, and the ego-centric study finds a persistent object-interaction gap despite scaling and targeted training changes.[9][10] Proposed research direction (interpretation): combine localized object/contact diagnostics with closed-loop task outcomes and separate changes in hardware, sites, participants, and control conditions.[6][10]

**Comparison quality is itself an open problem.** The retrieved sources do not provide matched datasets, compute, action spaces, or uncertainty estimates sufficient to rank latent control models, foundation video models, and interactive generators globally.[1][5][8][14][6] Apparent disagreements about whether 'better prediction' yields 'better control' partly concern different endpoints: one-step error, action alignment, visual consistency, physical observables, and task success.[13][7][6][9] Full-paper scrutiny, independent replication, and compute-normalized evaluation would strengthen these comparisons; they are recommended next steps, not completed checks in this survey.[8][14][9][10]
